# Riftlight: the campaign arc

**Status:** agreed direction for the November 2026 rewrite. Every Riftlight chapter is written
or revised against this page. It sits under [story.md](story.md) (the secret truth, the endings)
and [characters.md](characters.md) (who the Riftseekers are); where this page is more specific,
it wins for chapter writing. It does not change the campaign graph, its journey flags or its
endings.

**Why it exists.** In November 2026 the owner played *The Borrowed Ruins* and found it far below
Gemfall. Twelve chapters had lost their dice, their speakers and a third of their words to two
campaign-wide passes (see the chapter skill's
[worked example](../../../.claude/skills/chapter-cohesion/references/examples.md#riftlight-risk-converted-away)).
The design bible was sound, but no page said what each chapter adds to the whole: which beat it
lands, who carries it, and which piece of the mystery the party walks out with. Chapters drifted
into local chores, such as packing mats, filing reports and brushing boots. This page is that
missing ladder.

## The campaign in one breath

Snow falls from a clear sky at the Exchange, and the party follows it through a tear in the air
to the Glacier of Origins. Places are turning up where they do not belong. The Riftseekers want
to know why: Orin wants to understand it, Lady Olivia wants to use it. Wherever the party goes,
the land has been moved a little more. Each strand hands them one piece of an older map, and the
pieces fit: someone is putting the world back the way it was, long ago. At the glacier where it
began, they meet the one doing it, an old king who only wants to go home, and they decide what
becomes of him.

**The campaign question**, said in the departure chapter and asked again, in fresh words, at
the end of every chapter: *Something is moving the land. What is it, and can we put things
right?* Every chapter adds one answerable piece. No chapter answers the whole thing before the
finale.

## Three threads every chapter pulls

1. **The mystery (the clue trail).** Every chapter shows the party one thing out of place and
   lets them work out one true thing about it. The pieces grow, from a snowfall to a goat, a
   pass, a stone, a river, an island and a ruin. Three things recur so children can feel a
   pattern before anyone names it:
   - **The Listening Chime leans toward the glacier.** Near every moved place, the chime's hum
     points back toward the Glacier of Origins, faint at first and stronger as the campaign goes
     on. It is witnessed, never explained. It sets up the full-circle finale.
   - **Orin's map does not match.** At the departure, Orin shows the party his map with places
     that do not fit it, and asks them to remember anything else that does not match. Each key
     fragment is "one more piece that does not match today's map, but matches something
     older." These are knowledge, never items.
   - **Moved things try to go home.** Snow drifts back uphill, a goat keeps trying to walk back
     through a blinking opening, and the Bone Yard returns what is taken. This is the theme's
     image, and it pays off in Corvane's "I only want to go home."
   - Before the finale nobody names a person. The closest anyone comes is "whoever is moving the
     land" (Orin, at the convergence) and the reading room's "Do not shout near the old ice; you
     don't know what you might wake."
2. **The Riftseekers (the relationships).** Orin and Bram go carefully, Olivia and Pip want to
   harness it, and Seren sits between. Children should have an opinion about Olivia by the
   convergence. Pip is how they get one: he turns up in whichever strand they are in, notebook
   out, quoting "Lady Olivia says..." and trying her ideas, sometimes well and sometimes
   badly. His choice at the convergence mirrors theirs, so it has to feel earned.
3. **The theme (the choices).** *You can influence a great force but not command it.* Every
   chapter offers at least one choice between forcing something and understanding it first
   (push the island or shelter it, drive the wyrm off or move it, force the whales' opening or
   guide them). Neither side is punished, and both have real costs. Olivia is the voice of
   forcing; Orin the voice of understanding. The party is never lectured about which is right.

## The escalation ladder

Each rung is bigger than the last, so the world feels like it is getting stranger:

| Rung | What moved | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A snowfall, and a goat through a tear | Exchange, glacier |
| 2 | A river's edge, a pass that was not there last season | Shelf, washout, Cairnstep |
| 3 | A carved stone, a bridge's old stone, an island in the wrong sky, a town's door under glacier silt | Strand arrivals |
| 4 | A whole river down the wrong valley, whales lost between two openings, a bridge with no river | Strand middles |
| 5 | An island due back arriving early, memory ice showing the old river, ruins carried into the Bone Yard | Key nodes, strand ends |
| 6 | The pieces fit one old map: someone is doing this on purpose | Convergence |
| 7 | A whole region sliding back into place, and the old king himself | Finale |

## Recurring people

The opening chapters introduce the fellowship on every route (every party passes the
departure), so later chapters may greet Orin, Olivia and Seren as known. Everyone else is
introduced by the first chapter in which each way in proves the meeting. Where a party may arrive
cold, the text greets them in words that are true either way.

| Who | Figure | Introduced in | What they want from the party |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Master Orin Vale**, griffin scholar (he/him) | `cast/orin_vale` | departure (every route) | Notice what does not match his map, and do not force what you do not understand. |
| **Lady Olivia**, unicorn (she/her) | `cast/lady_olivia` | departure | Help her prove the openings can be used. She means well and is a little too sure. |
| **Seren Wisp**, kitsune (she/her) | `cast/seren_wisp` | departure | Learn to listen (she teaches Attune in the first strand entered). |
| **Pip Thistledown**, manticore scout (he/him) | `cast/pip_thistledown` | the first strand chapter a party plays, each introducing him | Be impressed; help him prove himself to Olivia. |
| **Bram Lock**, bigfoot guard (he/him) | `cast/bram_lock` | convergence | Keep everyone behind the line when Olivia tries her summoning. |
| **Old Ossley**, frogfolk boatman (he/him) | `cast/old_ossley` | washout or Ashspan | Ferry folk across while the bridge is broken. |
| **Puddle**, Skupper guide (she/her) | `cast/saltrot_guide` | saltrot-arrival (every Coast chapter follows it) | Get her town's door open, then help her keep travelers safe on flats that will not stay put. She carries the whole Coast strand. |
| **Tally**, dragonling stone keeper (she/her) | `cast/dragonling_stone_keeper` | boundary-stone-bank, and again, in words true either way, in stone-counters | Keep the count true: a counter's stones must never move, and they have. |
| **Elder Murmur**, yeti memory reader (she/her) | `cast/yeti_elder` | reading-room | Read the memory ice without waking it. |
| **Keeper Juniper**, kitsune island keeper (she/her) | `cast/island_keeper` | whale-road (the island always follows it) | Bring the whales and the island due back home safely. She carries the Sky's middle. |
| The yeti herder (glacier), Tamsin (griffin river line), Brask (griffin pass ledger), Captain Thimble, the noodle seller | as now | their chapters | Unchanged; keep them speaking. |

A local is never "Skupper guide" or "kitsune keeper" for a whole chapter. Give them a name, a
voice, a want and a funny habit, and let them speak in most scenes they are in. The chapter's
`voiceGuide.npcVoices` lists them.

## Node by node

**Beat** is the sentence that must be true when the chapter closes, whichever road the party took.
**Question** is what the goal asks; a goal is a question or a promise, never only a chore.
**Adds** is what this chapter puts on the clue trail. **Depth:** *rewrite* means build the
chapter again to the chapter skill's bar; *revise* means keep what works (checks, fights, class
rooms) and fix arc, people and voice, rewriting any part that does not meet the bar.

## The contract every chapter keeps

A rewrite may change every scene id, flag name and choice inside a chapter. These do not move:

- **The campaign graph** (`content/campaigns/riftlight.json`): nodes, edges, rift actions,
  route sets, buffs, facts and node `onComplete` effects. A chapter that needs a graph change
  says why in its report; it does not make the change itself.
- **Journey flags read elsewhere**, set exactly as today on every path that should set them:
  `glacier_rift_woken` (hidden rift, when woken), `ossley_rescued` (washout), the
  `attune_taught` gate-and-set in the three strand arrivals and stone-counters,
  `whales_guided` / `sky_followup_promised` (whale-road), `stone_route_priority`
  (stone-counters), `valley_crossed_high` (wrong-valley), the three `fragment_*_learned` keys
  (set on every success path of their key node), `orin_chime_shared` (buried-bridge, as a real
  choice), `island_messages_saved` / `island_warning_sent` / `island_relay_delayed`
  (island, read by the temple), `wyrm_ally`, `temple_island_settled`, `temple_damaged`,
  `helped_bram`, `olivia_continued`, `pip_stayed_back`, `manipulate_learned`, the ending flags,
  and every route set member (`ice_shelf`, `river_exit`). Arrival-mode flags
  (`sky_arrival_flight`, `saltrot_arrival_*`, `noticed_the_sky_ship`,
  `starweaver_read_first_rift`, `whales_scattered`, `olivia_trusted`) are set by the graph or
  earlier chapters; read them for variants and callbacks. Other journey flags a chapter sets today
  that nothing reads (the `memory_*` and most `island_*` bookkeeping) may be dropped; take
  them out of `graph.flags` only when the integration pass regenerates proofs.
- **Class rooms** (`classPlace`): the Rookery at the Exchange, the Conservatory at the glacier,
  the Dojo in sky-islands-arrival and the Academy at Ashspan, as
  `riftlight-class-rooms.test.ts` pins. The singing hall heals for free.
- **The Listening Chime** (`listening_chime`) is the party's only quest item from the departure
  on; no chapter grants another item that crosses a chapter.
- **Chapter metadata:** `id`, `campaignId`, `index`, `biome`, `music`, `xpAward`,
  `estimatedMinutes` (30, finale 40), encounter `map`s that exist in `content/maps/`, and art
  keys and cast figures that exist in `assets/manifest.json`. No new art is owed by a rewrite:
  reuse the chapter's and its strand's existing scene art and figures.
- **The finale's** board fights, anchors (2 to 3, capped), the three endings and their epilogue
  plates, and Olivia's epilogue variant, as [story.md](story.md#finale-and-endings--agreed) says.

## The bar each chapter is held to

The chapter skill (`.claude/skills/chapter-cohesion/SKILL.md`) is the bar. In short:

- A Gemfall-shaped evening: a spine (shortest success path) of at least 30 scenes, with 14 or
  more votes and checks, about 1,300 to 1,800 words on the spine, a quoted line in most scenes
  with someone on stage, and one concrete funny detail per scene.
- **Real risk.** Dice checks where the outcome is uncertain and both results go somewhere real,
  mostly things the party does. A few story interactions where the hands-on job suits the fiction,
  each with a miss that changes something. One or two fights where the conflict changes the
  situation. `cohesion.mjs shape` should read like a Gemfall row: checks in the high single
  digits or more, inert games one or two at most, echo votes rare.
- **Choices with consequences** that a later scene in the chapter, or a later chapter through the
  contract flags, actually reads. No gear-handling votes, and no bookkeeping tail after the
  climax.
- The goal question in scene 1, in the story's own words; the chapter's beat true at every
  success ending; the campaign question asked again at the close, with this chapter's piece added.
- Continuity: introduce before use on every way in (`content:continuity`, the concept sweep,
  `continuity.json`), at every merge.
- Voice: about eight years old, read aloud, warm and a little spooky, never scary, US spelling,
  no em dashes, never count the party.
