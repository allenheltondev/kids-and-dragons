# Rescue pair: planned playtest

Stuttering Flats and Bone Yard target thirty minutes each. These are design
budgets, not observed family timings. The pacing report omits authored interaction
time; automated routes establish correctness, not enjoyment or duration.

| Movement | Stuttering Flats | Minutes | Bone Yard | Minutes |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Find a workable approach | Read the blinking gap; choose mat or shell bank | 7 | Choose carried gear; observe returning objects | 7 |
| Learn through doing | Greet, sling and free the adult goat | 8 | Trace the return; support the passage; rest and plan | 6 |
| Commit to a plan | Signal the herder; execute or recover the crossing | 9 | Place carried mats and play the seam encounter | 11 |
| Leave something useful | Clear the bank, draw a warning and credit helpers | 6 | Recover own gear, take the actual exit and choose a report | 6 |
| Total | | 30 | | 30 |

Read-aloud, discussion, taking turns and the resulting activities share these
budgets. Do not impose waits or require extra attempts to meet them. Time the
shortest class-assisted route too: abilities intentionally save a job, and the
remaining rescue or encounter must still carry a satisfying session.

## Decisions that survive the merge

| Choice | Later playable consequence | Cost or limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flats: short mat approach / shell bank | Only the shell route retains the ramp option at the crossing | Shell markers and returning the ramp require carrying work |
| Flats: checked visual signal / guide-led signals | A correctly remembered signal unlocks a wider crossing target | Misses and assistance remain guide-led, never credited as independent recall |
| Flats: unicorn tending | A mended leg unlocks its own wider crossing | Ordinary drying remains a complete ungated rescue |
| Flats: sling crossing miss | A cooperative recovery replaces a retry | Goat reaches the herder; sling stays on the snowy side and the ending says so |
| Yard: carry mats / travel light | Carried mats cover the northern combat hazard, with real terrain effects | Placement and recovery take work; the southern hazard and enemies remain |
| Yard: mark seam / clear the way | The close-seam objective takes two turns instead of three | Marking is a shared preparation task, not an automatic victory |
| Yard: own plank / Yard stone / Brace | Own plank can be reclaimed after replacing its support; stone catches the plank; Brace saves the support task | Retreat leaves caught gear inside; there is no inner recovery menu after defeat |
| Yard: own guide line / arches | A prepared line permits a direct return after victory | Retreat uses the longer outer rim, earning passage completion there |
| Yard: relay / keep the account | Play the actual relay or retain a private party account | The guide retains her own observations; the ending does not invent a sent party report |

These equipment consequences are local expedition-kit state, not inventory item
transactions or promises of later campaign rewards. No Yard specimens leave.
All heroes stay on the Flats side while the adult goat reaches its herder.

## Family observation

Try solo, two-player and four-player tables, including relaxed/assisted controls.
Have children explain the gear tradeoff before choosing. Note where instructions
need repeating, where everyone contributes, and whether the next job feels
different. Read each ending against the gear actually recovered or left behind.
Keep the warning-making and account choices open to children's own contributions.

Record active play, breaks, reading and encounter time separately. Watch whether
recovery feels like a new plan or a chore. If either chapter runs short, deepen
useful discovery and discussion; if long, trim redundant explanation and handling.
Do not infer family timing from synthetic route counts.

[Issue #511](https://github.com/allenheltondev/kids-and-dragons/issues/511) tracks a
future family-session timer with durable timestamps, privacy choices and aggregate
pacing evidence. It is deliberately separate from this content wave.

## Verification

The route tests drive real interaction controls across gear, signal, class,
assistance and party-size combinations. Story-route combat outcomes are explicitly
simulated there; separate board tests play all four encounter variants through
objective victory and defeat, and compare covered-tile hazard damage. Schema,
continuity, campaign proof and type checks remain required before delivery.
