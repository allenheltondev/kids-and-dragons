# Continental Rift — story and spoilers

> **AUTHOR-ONLY SPOILERS.** This document contains truths players must not learn until the very end.

## Premise — agreed
Some time after Gemfall, a rift unexpectedly opens at the Exchange and deposits the party at the **Glacier of Origins**. Regions of the world are being displaced: fragments of terrain, creatures and entire areas appear where they do not belong. Players learn rift behaviors and how to navigate them through hands-on discovery, while pursuing local problems and exploring the world. Gemfall is a prerequisite: every party arriving here has completed it, with whichever ending it reached, and plays its Sworn characters.

Gemfall was the introduction to the game. Continental Rift increases gameplay progression: decisions, difficult combats, environmental challenges, conditional paths, revisits, and learned rift mechanics.

## Secret truth — for the finale only
An ancient emperor, **Corvane**, once ruled the land when its geography was different: the realm of **Pangea** ("all lands"), in which today's regions sat in a different arrangement. He was trapped for roughly a millennium. The events surrounding Gemfall's titan/seal have allowed him to escape, **regardless of which valid Gemfall resolution players chose**.

**Pangea as the clue trail — agreed.** Players discover the old world through **fragments that do not match today's map**. Early on, "Pangea" is **Orin's theory** that the land was once laid out differently: a pattern, not a person, so it reveals nothing about the emperor. At the end the name turns out to describe a real realm, Corvane's, and the clue the party has collected all along becomes the reveal. Canon already supplies the fragments:
- **Forgotten Paths:** the glacier memory of the river leaving by a different valley; the Red Sky Foothills boundary stones, older than the dragonlings and carved with an eruption no one's count matches.
- **Restless Coast:** the Saltrot's sealed door and buried bridge; ruins carried into the Bone Yard, where taken objects always come back.
- **Wandering Sky:** the island that is "due back".
- **Along the way:** the Plains' vanished ford; a route-song verse that matches no landmark; a Stone Crossing span stone that matches no local rock.

**Guardrails.** Pangea never explains the Expanse ("was not always like this" stays untouched), the Hollow Gate's "world before the red sky", or the Legend Dragon. Corvane wants **places** back and does not care about the sky, which keeps him apart from the Deep Hollow Cult, who want the sky's broken laws set right.

**His prison and the cause — agreed (creator-only canon, pending registration).** He was held in the **Glacier of Origins**. The eruption that opens Gemfall happens in every timeline, before any ending; its tremors started the glacier's changed melt, and the melt loosened his prison. This gives the existing canon tension "the melt is changing" a cause, which is why it is a deliberate canon decision. It explains why the first rift drops the party at the glacier, and it lets the campaign close where it began: a **full-circle ending** back at the glacier is the intended shape of the finale. It does not explain the summer crevasse, the glacier's memories or any other protected mystery; the glacier still never hands out complete historical answers.

The emperor is trying to restore the world to the arrangement of his former empire. He knows ancient rift magic and can **summon or influence** rifts, but the underlying force is more powerful than him. He **cannot choose a summoned rift's intrinsic behavior**. His attempts to bring back old geography are causing widespread unpredictable consequences. His intent is not simplistic evil: he wants his world back, but his actions threaten the present inhabitants of the realm.

**Revelation timing:** hide his existence, involvement, old map and true rift origins until the **very end** of the campaign. Most of the playthrough is about where to go, how to help, and how these strange rifts behave. Near the climax players may notice patterns indicating intentional influence, but should not be given explicit early exposition about an emperor.

The magical force itself need not be sentient; do not canonize a conscious rift deity or explanatory origin prematurely.

## Shape — agreed direction
- One strong shared prologue in the Exchange and Glacier.
- A nonlinear **story graph** with routes forward, sideways, backwards and changed revisits.
- Three interweaving regional strands, **not three mandatory checklists**.
- Different discoveries, allies and practical advantages based on route.
- A late revelation and a finale with meaningfully distinct choices ([below](#finale-and-endings--agreed)).
- **Size — agreed:** about 22–24 nodes authored, 14–16 played on a typical playthrough (roughly 1.5× Gemfall), each node 30–40 minutes. **The shortest valid route to the finale is 11 nodes:** `exchange-rift → glacier-survival → riftseekers-departure → hidden-glacier-rift → saltrot-arrival → buried-bridge → (back through the Saltrot hub, a pass revisit) → whale-road → the-island-due-back → squall-temple → convergence → finale`. It skips the Coast's strand end, because the convergence edge needs only both key nodes. So only 10 nodes pay before the finale: 9 ordinary nodes and the convergence.
- **XP — agreed:** awards are sized so every party arriving at Sworn (700) crosses Radiant (2,600) **at the convergence, one node before the finale** (spec §8.1), and no route reaches level 8 (3,500). The spread between the shortest and longest routes is five paying nodes, which is why the convergence carries the weight. XP pays on first completion only.

  | Node | First-completion XP |
  | --- | --- |
  | Every ordinary node | **140** |
  | The convergence (every route plays it; Radiant lands here) | **650** |
  | The finale | **140** |

  Shortest route: 9 × 140 + 650 = 1,910 before the finale. Longest (16 nodes): 14 × 140 + 650 + 140 = 2,750, ending near 3,450. A setback halves a node's award, so a run with setbacks can arrive at the finale just short of Radiant; spec §8.2 accepts that.
- **Acceptance test, once the graph is authored:** an executable check that the shortest and longest routes to the finale land between 2,600 and 3,500, so any later change to the graph or the awards is caught.

## Beginning — agreed
1. Exchange rift throws players to the Glacier of Origins.
2. Glacier survival and early Riftlight hints.
3. **Brief encounter with Riftseekers as they are leaving the glacier.** They are packing up; Orin offers a lead rather than a lecture, while Lady Olivia's attitude can be glimpsed.
4. **Orin's lead — agreed:** griffin trouble in **Frostfang Peaks** (canon: the griffins' homeland). An aerie's pass ledger records a pass that was not there last season, which fits canon's passes closing earlier. The pass is ***the Cairnstep***, marked by griffin watch cairns (new canon, pending registration). A griffin-species player has the natural way in with Skyward.
5. Players can follow the lead, discover a hidden rift by deliberately exploring, or fall into the river on the way. The way to the Cairnstep runs along an **ice shelf above the Great River** where it leaves the glacier. That is a traverse, not a river crossing: the glacier and Frostfang share a bank, and canon forbids inventing river crossings.
6. In the [headwaters washout](nodes/headwaters-washout.md), repeated dice rolls determine how far downriver the party is carried, with safe but different story outcomes. A rift is an alternative route under pressure.

## Regional strands — planned, not fully authored
### Wandering Sky
Sky Islands, kitsune and cloud whales. Naturally drifting islands are disrupted; whale migration/navigation patterns may help players recognize rift patterns. Cloud whales are gentle, immense navigational creatures, **not ordinary enemies or mounts**. Sky Island foundations have ancient seafloor connections (existing world canon); avoid giving away the emperor.

### Restless Coast
Sallow Wastelands are **brackish river-mouth coastal mudflats**, not desert. Saltrot guides, the Bone Yard, colossal unidentified remains and bone crawlers. Rift displacements alter the terrain and move creatures between habitats. Glacierflow/Great River runs south from the glacier to the coast; don't unrealistically wash the party from glacier to Bone Yard in one encounter.

### Forgotten Paths
Glacier of Origins, yetis and memory in ancient ice. Environmental puzzles and changed revisits. The ice contains subtle ancient anomalies; avoid exposing the whole ancient-world explanation prematurely.

**The hidden glacier rift — agreed.** Green + blue: it needs something first, then stays open. Its requirement is a **sung note**; the yetis carve singing halls, so a Songkeeper has a natural way in, and any party can learn the tune during glacier survival. It leads to **the Saltrot**, which gives canon's "unfamiliar glacier silt reaching the Saltrot" a cause (creator-only canon, pending registration). Because it is persistent, it is also the way back, which is what makes returning to the glacier meaningful.

**The hidden glacier rift is not the summer crevasse.** `canon/biomes.yaml` holds a creator-only secret: a crevasse below the oldest layer that preserves a summer, with green light and warm air. Keep the hidden rift somewhere else and never let its green Riftlight read as the explanation for that crevasse. Linking the two would be a canon decision, not an authoring one.

## Finale and endings — agreed
**1. The final rift.** At the glacier, where his prison was, Corvane opens his biggest rift yet, pulling a whole region back into Pangea's arrangement. **The fragments are knowledge, not items:** a memory, a buried bridge and a carved stone cannot be carried, but the party remembers them. Each fragment the party *learned* is one choice ("Anchor it with what the glacier remembered", the buried bridge, the island that came back), gated on that strand's **key node being completed** (a journey flag the key node sets), never on inventory. Each shows the land where it belongs **now** and collapses part of his rift. **Anchors are fragments plus allies, capped at 3:** an ally Olivia or an ally wyrm adds one, and both together still add only one ([characters.md](characters.md#how-the-partys-choices-decide-the-riftseekers--agreed)). With 2 anchors his rift is broken but he is still strong; with 3 it is overpowered and he starts the fight weakened. The fragments win because the land remembers its present shape, not because the party out-magics him, so the theme holds.

**2. The battle.** With his rift broken, Corvane fights to hold what is left open: Corvane plus displaced creatures pulled through his rift, among open rifts that act as hazards (#201). Manipulate and its class equivalents matter here: shutting a hazard rift, holding back the ice. The party reached Radiant one node earlier, so the climax is fought as the new form (spec §8.1). Two authored versions of the encounter, chosen by flag-gated choices on anchor count: standard, or Corvane weakened with one fewer creature. Defeat means his strength is spent and the rift collapses; nobody dies, and he stays sympathetic, an old man who wanted his home back.

**3. His fate — the endings.** History files the party under a name, as in Gemfall:

| Ending | Reached by |
| --- | --- |
| ***The Glacier Keepers*** — he is returned to the ice: "the ice keeps him, and he keeps the memory." The full circle. | Winning, then choosing it |
| ***The Ones Who Listened*** — he lives with the yetis as the one who remembers Pangea. | Winning, then choosing it |
| ***Rivers Wander*** (setback) — his pull half-works and places stay jumbled "for now", scoped to this campaign like Gemfall's Last Ring. The Riftseekers spread out to watch the rifts. The spark: Orin's "Then we learn the new map." | Losing the battle: a whole-party knockdown branches here rather than ending the run |

- **Lady Olivia is an epilogue variant, not a separate ending,** read from earlier flags (whether the party helped Bram stop her summoning; Pip's choice): **ally** (she helps hold the land), **rival** (she leaves to try her own way, unpunished) or **redeemed** (she joins Orin's study).
- In both wins the displaced places go home, so the shared canon map stays intact.
- Every ending completes the campaign and carries the characters over; reaching the setback limit still fails the attempt, as in Gemfall.
- **Campaign 3** must work whether Corvane sleeps in the ice or lives with the yetis, as this campaign works after every Gemfall ending.

## Theme — author guidance, not in-game exposition
You can influence a powerful natural force without being able to command its behavior. Curiosity, judgment, choices and consequences outweigh raw power. Lady Olivia and Orin respond to uncertainty in contrasting ways; the emperor's error is treating influence as complete control.

## Canon class hubs (#483)

The Rookery stays at the Exchange and the Conservatory stays on the Glacier of Origins.
The Dojo and its pillow lesson now sit off the sheltered rest path in `sky-islands-arrival`,
in the Sky Islands. Its keeper's welcome and edge-marking beat still finish that chapter.

`ashspan-bridge` spans the bridge and a village in the Whispering Marsh. After helping
travelers board, the party can stay at the ferry and discuss its onward route, or choose
"Visit the Academy in the marsh" and follow a posted raised footpath to fetch a dry reed
mat for the ferry deck. The village bench is a rest with a side door to the Academy
and its telescope lesson. Parties that visit carry the mat back to Ossley before the ferry-route
ending; parties that stay go straight to that ending, with both chapter goals complete. No hub remains at the glacier camp. The campaign keeps 24 nodes and its XP budget;
these visits are inside their host chapters. The singing hall still heals for free.

The ungated long bank road runs from the departure camp to Ashspan Bridge. An upper bank
road connects the bridge to Frostfang Shore and its Cairnstep path, where the griffins offer
a flight to the Sky Islands. These routes keep both relocated hubs reachable for every party,
including parties that decline a rift or lose the shelf crossing.
