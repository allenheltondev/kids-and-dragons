# Riftlight play and readability audit

The likely match for the reported bridge chapter is **A Bridge Without a River**
(`riftlight-buried-bridge`, index 5). The current Ashspan chapter is a short ferry
visit with no dice checks; its existing local revisions were left intact.

## Findings

The buried bridge had 84 authored scenes. Its normal path repeatedly asked for
one of two reasonable work plans, described the plan, rolled, described a result,
and repeated. Recording a conclusion, tidying the bank, and explaining findings
all required rolls. Alternate plans frequently merged immediately. Repeated
safety explanations and procedural language hid the actual mystery.

This structure also appears in the six regional investigations and the later
rescue/finale chapters. Existing pacing tests required fourteen decisions, six
checks and thirty minutes of estimated play. Those minima encouraged padding.
The game already supports authored interactions, but none of these twelve
chapters used them.

## Changes

The bridge is now a **roughly 30-minute expedition**, with 37 authored scenes
across its branches. The earlier 16-scene revision shortened it too far; the
owner clarified that the intended session length should stay around thirty
minutes. The expansion adds complications, experimentation and consequences.

1. Meet Orin; decide whether to share the chime readings.
2. Find firm ground and spend the single board on a fixed viewing rail or a
   portable kit sled. Build it with a relay or a balancing activity.
3. Investigate the arch, observe a returning tide pulse, and track Orin's
   glacier sample through a small sample-pot muddle.
4. When the tide reaches the low work shelf, choose what to save first: the
   drawings or the return path. Nobody is left in the water.
5. Use the prepared equipment. Saving drawings means rebuilding washed-out
   route markers; protecting the path means reconstructing wet notes, with
   the lost fine measurements explicitly omitted.
6. Explain the old river from the checked evidence.
7. Choose the careful copy's destination. A local copy leads to a marked
   viewpoint and a trial visit; a research copy leads to an evidence page,
   a wrapped-copy relay, and a closed work area awaiting a later visit.
8. Pack up and hear the specific outcome of the party's priorities.

Even the thornguard shortcut leaves ten required activities across at least
five different mechanics. Equipment affects the rescue controls; priorities
change the recovery task and quality of the notes; the copy decision changes
who gets help now and what visitors can do after the party leaves. A group can
also explicitly dismantle a rail into a carrying board if it changes its plan.
The equipment and record consequences happen within this chapter. The existing
choice to share chime readings still carries its campaign consequences.

The bridge keeps the four goal flags, the shut door, coast discovery, and XP
award. There are no dice checks to negate completed interaction work. The tide
is a narrated complication, not a real-time deadline. Every activity retains
retry, assistance and relaxed alternatives. See the [thirty-minute playtest
plan](../campaigns/continental-rift/buried-bridge-playtest.md) for the time budget
and family observations still needed.

The other changes replace 40 check scenes across eleven chapters (some scenes
are alternate approaches to the same moment):

| Chapter | New play |
| --- | --- |
| Stone Counters | Balance a loaded/empty cart; aim a retrieval loop or support a slate lift. |
| Whale Road | Assemble a note/arm greeting with a quiet pause; aim or time a kit retrieval. |
| Reading Room | Hold a cradle support or standing mat; balance a tray or save its slips together. |
| The Island Due Back | Retrieve mail by aiming or timing; hold the signal frame; time a gap observation. |
| The Wrong Valley | Balance a tool bundle; coordinate clearing the line and markers. |
| Stuttering Flats | Support the goat sling; practice the party's chosen wait/move signals. |

Cast, scene art, rift presentation, campaign routes, combat boards, rewards and
later callbacks remain in use. Activities use existing engine controls, retry,
relaxed play, solo props, shared clues and untimed helping hands. The duskrunner's
mail recovery and practiced island relay get a wider timing/aiming target.
New text uses concrete actions, short cues and occasional character/prop humor.
The remaining chapters retain their authored dilemmas and real-risk rolls;
this pass replaces repeated roll clusters rather than rewriting every line.

## Verification and authoring guardrails

- Continuity walking enumerates success, failure and assistance branches, as it
  already does for combat outcomes. New bridge evidence contracts describe the
  revised path and require river evidence before its conclusion and ending.
- Engine tests actually play every Riftlight interaction, solo and with three
  heroes, on success and assistance; aiming/timing/puzzle failures also continue.
- Regional pacing tests require varied activities on required paths and cap
  reading runs. Bridge tests play sixteen equipment/priority/copy/party-size combinations,
  including mistakes, and verify changed tasks, endings and every goal/discovery.
- Route tests explicitly distinguish interaction mistakes from failed dice rolls.
  Cooperative completion cannot randomly damage mail, wet a map, or undo help.
- The dice-only duration model does not measure interaction time. Chapter minute
  metadata is a planning estimate, not an observed family session.

For future chapters: state one concrete goal; introduce a boundary once; let
children do something with evidence; keep consequential choices; make routine
handover/cleanup a short shared beat. Do not fill a duration target with more
votes and rolls. Use a different activity when the fictional task changes.

Family playtesting remains necessary: check whether children can repeat the
one-sentence goal, share clues without adult translation, and want another turn.
The automated checks establish valid play and preserved consequences, not fun.
