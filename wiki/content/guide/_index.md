## The short version

1. **One device starts a game** and picks **Party** (TV and phones) or **Travel** (phones only).
2. **Everyone else joins** by typing the four letters on the big screen, or by scanning the QR code.
3. **Each player builds a hero**, or brings back one they played before, and taps **Ready**.
4. **Play the chapter.** The story is read out on the shared screen, and the choices appear
   on the phones as big pictures to tap. When something is uncertain, someone rolls the d20.
5. **About thirty minutes later** the chapter ends. Everybody gets the same XP, and the
   heroes keep everything they earned for next time.

There are no timers on any decision, nothing to read that a grown-up can't read aloud, and
nothing an eight-year-old has to type. Any screen can be refreshed at any time without
losing the game.

## Who it is for

Kids & Dragons was made for one family: two grown-ups and an eight-year-old. Three players is
what it is tuned for. The fights, the number of enemies, and the turn order all assume a party
of three.

**Nobody dies.** A hero who runs out of HP is *knocked down*, and a friend can help them up. If
the whole party is knocked down, the fight is lost, but the story keeps going down a different
road. There is no game-over screen anywhere in the game.

## Accounts are optional

You can play without signing in. A household that never signs in is cleared after a week
without play. At the end of a chapter the game offers to **keep your heroes**, shown as a
lantern with the party inside as lights. Keeping them means signing in with a code sent by
email (and, if you like, a passkey). There is no password anywhere.
