## Lore

> *Nobody in the flock has ever had the first idea. The flock has had all of them.*

Sheeple graze the low ridges of the Plains in flocks of anywhere from three to three hundred, and nothing a sheeple does was its own idea. One starts to walk, and the rest walk. One sits down to watch the sunset, and the hillside sits down. Ask a sheeple who decided and it will point, sincerely, at the sheeple next to it, who will point at the next. None of them has a name to give you; a sheeple is "the one in the tall hat" or "the one by the stream" for exactly as long as that is true, and the flock has never needed more. The flock still gets somewhere. Things get decided, songs get sung, and outfits get invented. Where the first idea comes from is the oldest question on the Plains, and the sheeple are the least bothered by it. When a flock comes apart it splits into threes, never ones or twos, and each trio lifts its head and lets out the Gather: a single enormous call that carries across three valleys and means one thing only, come here. No ear but a sheeple's can hear it. A traveler sees three sheeple throw back their heads in perfect silence, and a few minutes later the hillside fills with sheeple.

### For Fun

- Follow-the-follower: a long, slow ring of sheeple walking around a hilltop, each following the one in front. It has gone on since spring. Nobody started it.
- Shearing Day. The whole flock shears on the same morning, because one of them did, and by evening it has felted a matching new wardrobe of hats, rings, and tassels that looks like nothing anyone has worn before.
- Copying the clever. A flock that once camped beside a particularly sharp centaur spent a month solemnly reading each other's scarves the way she read sashes.
- Leaning with the wind. When the grass bends, the flock bends, and it calls that dancing.

### Getting Along

Friendly, warm, and skittish. Meeting a flock starts with a full minute of silence while every sheeple waits for another to say hello, and then twenty hellos at once. They get close quickly and bolt even faster. A shout, a snapping branch, or a jackalope in the grass sends a flock scattering in threes. Then come the Gather calls, the regathering, and a sheepish return that pretends nothing happened. The way to befriend them is to be still, gentle, and patient. Most strangers they simply like. A stranger who is truly clever is different: the flock knows it at once, without a word said, and starts doing whatever that one does.
